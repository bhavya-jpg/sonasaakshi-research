# Competitive landscape

| Product | What it does for job work | Whose side |

| Notebook and issue slip | The default today | Each side keeps its own |

## The gap: what none of them do

| Gap | Why it matters | SonaSaakshi |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The karigar has no record he controls | The problem statement requires that each karigar has full access to his own account | Karigar-owned ledger across all his jewellers |
| No two-party signature | One side's notebook can be disputed; neither can be proven | Every event co-signed and hash-chained |
| Text and English UI | Many karigars are Bengali or Hindi speakers with limited literacy | Voice in, voice back, in each party's language |
| No deadline planning | Missed wedding dates are a named cost | Planner checks each karigar's open load against the occasion date |
| No dispute protocol | Scale disagreements are settled by power | Neutral re-weigh at a JanAI Cafe or refiner, both readings logged |
| No portable reputation | The brochure's "following the money" hint | Work Passport, shared with a lender only on consent |

## Positioning line

"Tally digitises the jeweller's notebook. SonaSaakshi gives both sides one notebook they can both sign, in their own language."

We integrate rather than compete: export job cards in a format the jeweller's ERP or accountant can import.
