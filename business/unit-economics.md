# Unit economics (assumptions to validate)

## Who pays

| Payer | What they pay for | Price (assumption) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Jeweller | Fewer unexplained losses, on-time orders, fewer disputes | Rs 1,499 per shop per month |
| Karigar | Nothing | Free; his side drives adoption across his jewellers |
| Lender (later) | A consented, signed work history to underwrite a karigar | Referral fee per disbursal, simulated in the hackathon |
| JanAI Cafe | Earns rather than pays | 20% of subscriptions it signs and services |

## Worked numbers

| Line | Calculation | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Value of 1 g of 22K gold | Rs 1,26,150 per 10 g (Bengaluru, 15 Nov 2026) | Rs 12,615 |
| Grams a month that pay for the subscription | 1,499 / 12,615 | 0.12 g |
| Example shop loss (assumption) | 1,000 g issued x 0.3% unexplained | 3 g = Rs 37,845 a month |
| Value of the demo issue | 52.340 g x Rs 12,615 | Rs 6,60,269 |
| SAM (assumption) | 1,94,039 registered jewellers x 30% using job work x Rs 17,988 a year | About Rs 105 crore ARR |
| Cafe operator income (assumption) | 40 jewellers x Rs 300 (20% of 1,499) | About Rs 12,000 a month |

## Karigar benefit (to measure, not claim)

- Days from delivery to payment: today (field data) vs. with two-party settlement.
- Disputed grams per month.
- Lender offers received on a shared Work Passport (pilot only).

## Open questions for the field

1. Will jewellers pay monthly, or prefer a per-job fee?
2. Does the jeweller or the karigar resist the shared record more?
3. Can a karigar association introduce the app to its members, so it doesn't arrive as "the jeweller's app"?
